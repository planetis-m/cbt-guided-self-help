# Clinical Basis

Use this reference to understand why the workflow is structured as guided
self-help rather than psychotherapy. It summarizes sources; it is not a
treatment manual.

## Design Conclusions

- CBT is structured, present-focused, goal-oriented, active, and collaborative.
- A typical session includes a check-in, bridge from the prior session,
  collaborative agenda, work on a focused problem, an action plan, summary, and
  client feedback.
- CBT maps interactions among situations, thoughts or beliefs, emotions,
  physical sensations, and behavior.
- Guided discovery should help the person examine and test their own
  interpretations rather than accept the facilitator's interpretation.
- Between-session practice and routine progress review are central.
- Written thought records can be kept for later review. They capture the
  situation, feelings, thoughts, evidence considered, an alternative thought,
  and any change in feelings.
- A final session summary should capture the most important lessons from the
  session.
- Digital or written guided self-help is a lower-intensity intervention. It has
  less capacity for individual adaptation than clinician-delivered treatment
  and should not be treated as equivalent to it.
- Treatment selection, diagnosis, specialized protocols, and higher-intensity
  CBT require trained and supervised practitioners.

## AI Adaptation

The workflow adapts the repeatable, low-intensity parts of CBT:

- transparent structure
- collaborative agenda setting
- a simple CBT map
- gentle guided discovery
- low-risk behavioral activation or problem solving
- a small human-chosen action plan
- progress review and session feedback

It deliberately does not adapt clinical formulation, diagnosis, treatment
selection, specialized protocols, high-intensity interventions, or management
of severe presentations. This boundary is an inference from the clinical
guidance's distinction between guided self-help and interventions delivered by
trained, supervised practitioners, combined with current AI safety guidance.

## Sources

Accessed 2026-06-04.

1. NICE, **Depression in adults: treatment and management (NG222)**:
   guided self-help uses structured CBT/behavioral activation/problem-solving
   materials with support and outcome review; it is structured, goal-oriented,
   and focused on current issues. The guideline also notes its lower capacity
   for individual adaptation and that more therapist contact should be
   considered for more severe depression.
   <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222/chapter/Recommendations>

2. NICE, **Generalised anxiety disorder and panic disorder in adults (CG113)**:
   uses stepped care; guided self-help should be CBT-based, supported by a
   trained practitioner, and include progress/outcome review; high-intensity
   CBT should be delivered by suitably trained and supervised people following
   empirically grounded protocols.
   <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg113/chapter/Recommendations>

3. NHS, **Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)**: describes assessment before
   CBT, discussion of situations and links among thoughts, feelings, and
   actions, trying alternative approaches, between-session practice, worksheets
   or diaries, and maintenance planning.
   <https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/talking-therapies-medicine-treatments/talking-therapies-and-counselling/cognitive-behavioural-therapy-cbt/overview/>

4. Beck Institute, **What Are CBT Sessions Like?**: describes identifying
   values and goals, collaborative agenda setting, evaluating thoughts and
   beliefs, action plans, progress measurement, transparency, and end-of-session
   feedback.
   <https://cares.beckinstitute.org/about-cbt/what-are-sessions-like/>

5. U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for
   Depression: Therapist Manual**: describes the common session sequence of
   mood check, bridge, agenda, review, focused discussion, summaries, action
   assignment, final summary, and feedback. It says the final summary may be
   given by the therapist or the person and should cover the most important
   lessons from the session.
   <https://www.mirecc.va.gov/docs/cbt-d_manual_depression.pdf>

6. Dattilio and Hanna, **Collaboration in cognitive-behavioral therapy**,
   Journal of Clinical Psychology (PubMed record): describes collaborative
   empiricism as therapist and patient working together on shared goals and
   empirical examination.
   <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23616295/>

7. NICE, **Quality statement 2: Decision making**: supports shared decision
   making and keeping the person at the center of care decisions.
   <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/qs14/chapter/quality-statement-2-decision-making>

8. NHS Every Mind Matters, **Thought record**: describes a written thought
   record as something a person can refer back to later and structures it
   around the situation, feelings, thoughts, evidence for and against,
   alternative thoughts, and changed feelings.
   <https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/self-help-cbt-techniques/thought-record/>
